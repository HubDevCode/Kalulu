## Kalulu 2.5.3

### Windows: songs start in a second or two instead of about 20

On Windows every YouTube or YouTube Music track waited about 20 seconds to start. The bundled `yt-dlp.exe` is a frozen program that unpacks itself on every call, and Windows Defender scans it each time, for every track and every prefetch.

The installer now also carries the official embeddable Python and the yt-dlp zipapp. Kalulu loads yt-dlp once, as it does on Linux, and asks it for each track: a song starts in a second or two, and the next ones are ready in advance. yt-dlp is loaded as Kalulu opens, so the first song does not wait either. The bundled yt-dlp still updates itself once a day.

### Faster song start on Windows and Linux

- A click no longer waits behind the songs resolved in advance: before, a song the pointer had just passed over sat in the low-priority queue, and the click joined it there.
- yt-dlp stays loaded for 15 minutes without requests (before: 90 seconds), so a song after a pause does not reload it.
- The song under the pointer is resolved after 150 ms (before: 350 ms), so it is usually ready by the click.
- One request less per song: the HLS and DASH manifests, which Kalulu never plays, are no longer downloaded.

The quality does not change: Kalulu still plays YouTube's best audio, Opus at about 130 kbps.

### Updates are checked at every start

Kalulu now checks for a new version every time it starts (before: once a day). When one is out, an **Update available** panel shows the version, the date and the notes, with **Download and verify** and **Later**. The check can be turned off in Settings → Updates.

### Steady play button on the Home cards

On the Home page, the play button of a song, album, artist or playlist card blinked while the pointer was on it, so a click often missed and had to be repeated. Moving onto the button made the card think the pointer had left, which hid the button, which brought it back. The button now stays steady while the pointer is on the card or on the button, and hides once the pointer leaves both.
